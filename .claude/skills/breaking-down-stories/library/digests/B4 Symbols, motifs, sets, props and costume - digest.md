# Digest B4: Symbolism, motif systems, props, sets, costume and makeup

Source: `research/B4_symbolism_motif_production_design.md` (1,004 lines). Brackets: P# = principle (§1), R# = decision rule (§8), § = section, Ex = worked example (§11), M## = *The Catch* register entry (§9.3). Caps marked [practice] are working rules, not sourced numbers.

## 1. Scope

1. Turns abstract meaning (theme, inner state, relationship) into filmable things (objects, places, clothes, body marks, repeated sounds), with rules, a checklist and a mistakes list against cliché and heavy-handedness.
2. Builds a **motif register** (meaning, plant, develop, reveal, payoff, states, framing) that doubles as the continuity record for props, costume and makeup; full register for *The Catch*, brief for *The Long Places*.
3. Runs after A2's scene breakdown, before B1 (shots) and B2 (light); feeds C2's asset sheets. Color meanings belong to B2 §8.4, mirror state and eras to C2 §7.

**Terms** (§0.3). *Motif*: a concrete thing (object, place, color, gesture, sound) that returns and gathers meaning. *Emblem*: a character's signature object. *Objective correlative*: things shown that produce a specific emotion. *Metonymy*: a nearby thing stands in (empty clip for the puck); *synecdoche*: a part for the whole. *Core opposition*: the theme's two poles as nouns. *Hinge*: a motif that crosses poles. *Plant* (first clear sight) → *develop* (state, owner, surroundings or audience knowledge changed) → *reveal* (hinges: the meaning learned) → *payoff* (meaning spent) → *coda* (one quiet return, L0–L1). *Teaching shot*: shows how a thing works before a payoff needs it. *Rhyme*: repeating an earlier composition (side, height, size, light). *Eyeline chain*: shots following a gaze object to object. *Plot machinery*: needed by the plot, on neither pole. *State*: physical condition (whole, broken, empty, bloodied, reversed, burnt). *Emphasis*: L0 Present, L1 Placed, L2 Featured, L3 Spent; sound S0 Bed, S1 Clear, S2 Foreground, S3 Alone. *Set loudness*: the Affrons' levels denotation, punctuation, embellishment, artifice, narrative (glosses are the file's reading).

## 2. Rules

**Harvest and restraint**
1. [P1, R4, R27, §2.2] If a beat's inner state (A2) has no thing carrying it, then use, in order: an action with an object already in the scene; the actor alone; last, a new object with a practical job, placed at L0 in an earlier scene and flagged as an invention, because a symbol arriving just when needed reads as the author's hand. If the emotion dwarfs the things shown, add action or let the actor carry it, never decoration.
2. [P4, R22] If an object makes sense only as a symbol, then give it a practical reason or cut it; a character explains a metaphor only with a practical purpose (Saye teaching physics), because a recognized symbol goes inert.
3. [R5, R25, §7] If a thing's meaning comes from general culture (doves, clocks, crosses) or it would fit any film with this theme (wilting plant, ticking clock, rain), then replace it (unless the story rebuilds its meaning from scratch) with a thing only this story has, with a detail from the text, because specificity separates motif from cliché. The translation table (§7) asks questions; it is never a lookup.
4. [R24] If an image is a known cliché (hands on glass, lone lit window, empty chair), then keep it only if the text names it, it has a practical job and it changes between appearances, and play it plainly (no slow motion, score swell or push-in), because only the story's specifics rescue a familiar image.
5. [§2.3, §2.6] If the story is realistic, then prefer in-world metaphor and metonymy to cut-based metaphor, and spend emphasis on the object carrying a character's risk or choice, never the MacGuffin, because the literal meaning arrives first and cut metaphor reads as editorializing.

**Ranking**
6. [§3.3, R1] If ranking a thing, then score the six tests (P3): 5–6 spine candidate, 3–4 supporting, 1–2 dressing at L0. Overrides in order: all appearances in one scene → single-scene prop; high score but on neither pole → plot machinery (frame for clarity); fails Source → at most supporting, flagged. Because only a thing that changes state and is handled carries a spine.
7. [§3.2, practice] If counting spines, then per channel: visual 3–5 for a short film (under ~40 pages), 5–8 for a feature, plus ≤1 sound and ≤1 body; a body mark needing its own insert more than once counts as visual. Supporting motifs stay ≤L2 except at their own payoff if it is a plot beat; if they outnumber spines more than 2:1, demote the weakest to dressing. Because extra spines compete for the same beats.
8. [§3.3, R3] If candidates exceed the cap, then keep hinges, then most state changes, then most handled; if two spines share a payoff moment, merge them or move one to a neighboring beat, because motifs peaking together cancel.
9. [R2] If there are several hinges, then the film's largest payoff (longest hold, strongest contrast in one visual component, within rule 17) goes to the one whose change falls closest to the climax, because that change is the theme happening physically.

**Emphasis and framing**
10. [P5, §3.1 step 9] If assigning L3, then ≤1 per motif (a second only as a deliberate inversion), on the payoff or a hinge's reveal, never the plant; ≤1 per turning point; ≤2 per scene, on different turning points with a lower shot between; S3 once per sound motif. Because emphasis is a budget.
11. [P6, R6] If it is a plant, then L0–L1, clear by composition and light, not size. If the plant is itself a plot event (rung breaks, wound made, sign read aloud, rule taught), use the level the event needs, ≤L2, with nothing pointing forward (no held pause, extra light, sound cue), because the audience must recognize it later without being told.
12. [§3.4] If building a motif, then plant low and pay off high, or by exact rhyme, or (hinge) L3 at the reveal and a quiet L1–L2 final payoff; change the neighbor in frame between appearances. L0–L1 get scene light only; an L3 light change needs a motivating source.
13. [R7, R8, R9] If a payoff needs to know how a thing works, then one L2 teaching shot first; if a body remembers, then repeat the plant's composition exactly; if the meaning is an absence, then frame the holder with a character's look leading to it; because suspense needs knowledge, the eye must remember with the body, and absence needs a frame.
14. [R10] If a non-speaking or faceless character must understand, then build an eyeline chain (character, object, second object), because (Kuleshov) the sequence carries the thought.
15. [R11] If two spine motifs share a shot, then only one goes above L1, because two loud objects split the eye.
16. [R21] If dialogue states an object's meaning, then keep the object L0–L1 during the line; reading a label aloud is not stating meaning (rule 11 applies). Because saying and showing at full volume is heavy-handed.
17. [R23] If a beat is L3, then sort: (a) **script markers** (on-screen state change, a line about it, a look or touch), never removed; (b) **framing** (size, held frame, rhyme); (c) **added signals** (music cue, light change, sound change, slow motion, an unmotivated camera move toward it; a move following a walk or search is staging). Any script marker → zero added signals; none → at most one. Because stacked signals are melodrama.
18. [R26] If a motif has paid off, then later appearances stay L0–L1 with ≤1 coda; a readout the plot needs may reach L2, with no rhyme, hold or signal recalling the payoff, because a loud return explains it twice.
19. [§2.5, §3.1 step 8] If a thing is an emblem or spine, then fix its look (silhouette, color, material, size), readable wide and close and distinct in silhouette in its scenes; any invented object must belong to one pole's shape family, because emblems must be recognized instantly and never drift.

**States and continuity**
20. [P2, R12] If a motif returns, then change its state, owner, neighbor or audience knowledge, because unchanged repetition is wallpaper.
21. [§5.3, R13, R14] If a state changes, then only on the beat that means it, holding in every later shot, wides included (or across a cut the audience accepts). Record the side of every sided detail (ring, wound, sleeve, half-smile) in the state table and C2's mirror tag per scene. If a character is damaged more than once and sides are unfixed, cluster the damage on one side (flag it) and keep the other clean for an emblem.
22. [R15, R16] If an object is destroyed, show its last state clearly first; if it returns after a long gap, keep it identical except the intended change, because the audience must know what is lost and recognition depends on sameness.
23. [§5.1] If dressing is picked up, it becomes a prop with a register line; a prop seen close and used roughly needs a hero version and duplicates per state (AI: one reference image per state), because states must match shot to shot.

**Sets and costume**
24. [§4.1, R17] If designing locations, then set each one's loudness first: at most two loud sets (short) or three (feature). Loud: one establishing wide at its first scene showing the governing feature, then shoot for action. Quiet: wides show only positions and danger; no shot exists only to show the set. Because a film can only have a few loud rooms.
25. [§4.4, R18] If a set changes state, then repeat a first-state composition for the second state and log both; if two institutions are morally paired, then rhyme their architecture, because the change must read as this place changing and the equivalence is felt before it is said.
26. [R19, R20] If a costume changes, then tie it to a turning point or forced circumstance, never variety; if a reveal is that a character is not what they seemed, then plant its mechanism (latches, seams) at L0, because unmotivated changes read as fashion and a fair reveal needs visible evidence.
27. [§6.1] If describing a working character's clothes, then name the wear ("faded at the seams, knees rubbed pale"), because models default to new clothes. Damaged costumes: one reference per stage, named with its first scene. Wounds: stages (fresh, bleeding, dressed, healing, scarred) in story time.

**Other**
28. [§2.7] If adapting prose, then a thing may replace a narrated sentence; in a screenplay keep the dialogue and let the thing add subtext.
29. [§2.10] If citing Van Sijll, then only convention names and films from her contents list. Block: a thing gets louder by contrast in one component; save the largest contrast for the payoff.
30. [M10, §14.2] If a motif is a sound, then record it once, change only the mix, never score over it, lay it in during editing.

## 4. Procedures

**P1. Order of work** [§0.1]: P2 steps 1–8 → set, prop, costume passes → state table to C2 → a THINGS field per shot → checklists → model wording.

**P2. Ten steps** [§3.1]: 1 theme as a question (not a moral); 2 core opposition as two nouns (+ secondary); 3 harvest everything named more than once or at a turning point, incl. sounds, words on things, body marks, with scene and what happens; 4 sort onto poles or mark hinges (most valuable); 5 test and rank; 6 one-line meaning and direction per spine and supporting motif; 7 map appearances (scene, state, owner, emphasis, framing: size, position, light, focus, handling; audience knowledge); 8 design the look (silhouette, color from B2, material, size), then one shape and material family line per pole; 9 check the budget (rule 10); 10 hand off: state table, one C2 asset sheet per state of each spine motif, register IDs in shot specs.

**P3. Six tests** [§3.3], one point each: Source (named, or an obvious property of something named); Physical (seen or heard without a caption); State (changes at least once); Link (a change coincides with a turning point); Handling (touched, looked at, acted on); Removal (cutting it loses a beat or a plant).

**P4. Register entry template** [§9.2] (exact): "**Meaning** (one line) and its direction of travel; **Plant**, **Develop**, **Reveal** (hinges only), **Payoff** with scene, exact line, and emphasis level (L0 to L3 for things seen, S0 to S3 for sounds); **States**; **Framing and rules**." Quotes verbatim, checked against their scene. Film header [§9.1]: theme question; oppositions; items per pole; shape families; hinges; ranks with per-channel counts; emphasis-3 map.

**P5. Show, don't tell** [§2.7], per inner-state beat: (1) subtext in one sentence; (2) Action with a thing; (3) Harvest the nearest thing; (4) the State matching the feeling; (5) the lowest Emphasis that registers; (6) Words (rule 28).

**P6. Costume, five questions** [§6.1], before describing clothes: Choice; Means and job; History (age, who bought, worn, mended, washed); Events (blood, tears, lost sleeve, borrowed blanket); Imposed identity (wristband, printed suit, label). Answer in nouns, materials, states; the outfit follows. Variables: silhouette, color, texture, fit, wear, damage, coverage, body marks.

**P7. State table** [§9.4]: a costume plot merged with props and makeup; rows = items, columns = scene ranges; unconfirmed sides marked "rec."; era per C2 §7.3.

**P8. Set pass** [§4]: loudness (rule 24); space variables (tendencies; context can reverse); dressing as history (wear, absences, one kept living thing); change type (revisited, rhymed, changed by a character, emptied).

**P9. User loop** [§0.2]: "Run the B4 motif pass on my script and show me the register"; the user reads only **Meaning** lines and corrects them; the LLM re-derives framing. Too heavy → "run Section 13 and rule 25 on its own register". Too generic → "Which of these motifs would fit any film with this theme?"

## 5. Checklists [§12] (a "no" needs a fix or a written reason)

**Film:** theme as question, opposition as two nouns; every spine on a pole or crossing; ≥1 hinge, holding the largest payoff; spines within caps; a shape family per pole, inventions inside one; written emphasis-3 map; all harvested, inventions flagged; meanings user-approved.

**Motif:** plant, develop, payoff each with scene, line, level; plant L0–L1 (≤L2 plot event, nothing forward); payoff louder, an exact rhyme, or quiet after a hinge's L3 reveal; post-payoff L0–L1 (L2 for readouts), ≤1 coda; any-film test; every return changes something; teaching shot if needed; look fixed and distinct; ≤1 L3.

**Scene:** each inner-state beat has a carrier or "actor alone"; L3 and S3 budgets (never two L3 in consecutive shots); practical reasons; loudness and dressing history; costume and makeup match the state table, sides included; object L0–L1 while dialogue states its meaning.

**Shot:** THINGS lists every register item with state and level; sides and mirror state right; nothing key too small for output resolution; added signals at L3 = 0 if the script marks the beat, else ≤1; no meaning words or mood adjectives in the prompt.

**Mistakes** [§13] (spot → fix): announced symbol → practical job; borrowed meaning (doves, ravens, broken mirrors, chess) → build it in-story; insert overkill (>1–2 per scene) → plants to L0–1; stacked signals → marker and framing only; telegraphing (plant bigger than payoff) → lower plant, raise or rhyme payoff; wallpaper → ration; motif drift → rewrite the meaning or cut; orphan plant or payoff → add or cut; explained metaphor → cut the line; everything symbolic (>2 loud sets); dressing that lies ("who bought this, when, why still here?"); costume as fashion → age to events; continuity break → run the state table, regenerate from the right reference; cliché played big; symbol replaces action → object in hand at the choice; stock shorthand (any register item with no quoted text line); glowing object → scene light; loud after payoff → L0–1 or cut.

## 6. Saying it to AI models [§14]

Vendor guides and practitioner experience, not tests; retest.

**Followed:** concrete nouns with material, color, size ("a flat matte-black disc the size of a hockey puck, no markings, clipped under it with a metal spring clip"); physical wear ("one bright polished band along its edge where arms have rubbed it for years; the rest dull"); position and size in frame, which is how to set a level; one saturated accent in a muted scene.

**Partly:** short prop text; exact text ("Goods only. No persons.", "IONA VALE") is a composited graphic (C2). States hold only with a reference per state. Fine hand work: stills beat video; use Blender, real footage or start and end frames. Native audio cannot repeat the pump's rhythm: record once.

**Ignored or misread:** meaning words ("symbolizes", "represents", "a metaphor for", "motif", "foreshadowing", "Chekhov's gun") are ignored or pull in stock imagery; mood adjectives ("ominous", "mysterious", "important", "symbolic") produce glowing, centered, hero-lit objects; named absences get drawn, so describe what is there ("a bare metal spring clip closed on nothing"; "bare white fridge door; the only object is a pot of mint on the windowsill") and use a negative prompt field; body left/right fails (check every frame; inpaint or composite); mirrored writing gets "corrected" (use a flipped insert graphic).

**Phrasebook** [§14.4]:
- M02 sill, L1: "Side view, medium close: a woman's forearm resting along a steel ledge that has one bright polished band worn along its edge; her cheek against wet brick; lit only by a flashlight held in her mouth"
- M05 empty clip, L2: "Extreme close-up: a man's fist gripping a small steel vacuum flask; beneath it a bare metal spring clip, closed on nothing; dark background"
- M11 label: "A small clear plastic container on a metal shelf; inside, a stiff strip of blue cloth stained dark brown; above it a handwritten label, letters drawn slowly and evenly like a careful copy of a picture" (composite the exact letters)
- M01 red: "Muted grey-black wet brick tunnel; the only bright color is a small red plastic safety tag wired to the cage gate"
- L0: "in the background, small, slightly out of focus". L3, pick one: "fills the frame" / "extreme close-up"; "held still, centered, the only sharp thing in the frame"; or the plant's exact composition. "Slow push in to the object" only if the entry calls for a move, never with music on the same beat. S3: an editing note, not a prompt.
- M17 reveal: "Medium close, camera still: the chest plates of a tall matte-black machine body swung open like two doors; inside, clear tubes, a small metal mount and a forearm-length glass vessel of dark water; the mount and vessel are the only sharp things; lit only by the same dim light as the rest of the room, no light coming from inside"
- Permit: "a folded official paper, creased soft and slightly curved, taken from the inside pocket of a linen jacket"
