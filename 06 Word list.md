# Word list

Stage uses one plain word for each thing, always the same one. The examples come from The Catch, most from scene 10 (the folder **09 Example - The Catch, scene 10**).

## The story

| Word | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| beat | The smallest change between people: one acts, the other reacts. | Scene 10 has 11 beats; in beat 7 Iona chews the leaf and says "Not mint." |
| turn | The beat where a scene's situation flips. | Beat 7: Iona learns that she has changed, not the world. |
| part | A stretch of a scene with its own turn. | Scene 10: finding out what happened, then keeping everyone in the room. |
| pause | A stop in the talk; where a script writes "(beat)", about a second. | After Iona steps between Saye and Eli, a long pause of 3 seconds with only the room's sound. |
| want | What a character is after in this scene. | Saye wants to find out what has happened to them before anyone leaves. |
| life want | What a character is after across the whole story. | Iona: to keep the people she is responsible for alive and together. |
| tactic | What a line or act does to the other person, as an "-ing" word. | Saye is probing; Jude is joking. |
| task | What the hands do while people talk. | Saye cuts the shirt away and cleans the wound. |
| story point | A moment named by its scene and a quotation. | Scene 24, "She deletes the way home." |
| group of scenes | Scenes worked on together. | Group 3, "the wrong world": scenes 7 to 10. |
| climax | Where the story's main question is settled for good. | The Catch: scenes 26 and 27. |
| tone | The feeling a scene or film is played in; the film's own is its home tone. | The Catch's home tone is tense. |
| scene intensity | How much pressure a scene carries, from 1 to 10 (a beat's goes from 1 to 5). | Scene 10 is at 6. |
| motif | A thing that comes back and gathers meaning. | The mint; the rings; the flask. |
| plant and payoff | A thing shown early, quietly, and the moment it matters. | The mint on the sill (shot 030) pays off at "Not mint." |

## The picture

| Word | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| shot sizes | How much of a person fills the frame, from far to near: extreme wide, wide, medium wide, medium, medium close-up, close-up, extreme close-up; an insert shows a thing close. | Shot 190 is wide; shot 150 a close-up; shot 090, Saye's ring, an insert. |
| turn shot | The shot where a scene turns. | Shot 150. |
| turn picture | The one frame a turn must show, written before any shot. | Iona's face as the taste changes. |
| setup | Where the camera stands, named "camera A", "camera B". | Camera A is far back along the table. |
| camera move | The camera itself travelling or turning. | In scene 10 the camera never moves. |
| floor-plan move | A character walking on the scene's floor plan. | Saye goes to the sill for the mint. |
| angle and height | How the camera tilts, and how high it stands. | Level, at Iona's eye height. |
| point-of-view shot | A shot seen through a character's eyes. | Shot 040 comes close: Saye's hands, the way Iona sees them. |
| whose scene | The person whose experience the scene follows. | Scene 10 follows Iona. |
| frame-left, frame-right | Left and right as the audience sees the picture. | Iona stands frame-left in shot 080. |
| own left, own right | A person's own hands and sides. | Iona's skinned palm is her own right. |
| hand nearest the camera | The hand closer to us. | Both women raise it in shot 080. |
| the line | The imaginary line between two people; while the camera stays on one side of it, each keeps a side of the frame. | The line runs across the table between Iona and Saye. |
| travel | Which way someone crosses the frame. | Nobody crosses the frame in shot 150. |
| does | What we see the body do, never a feeling word. | "Chews slowly; stops chewing; a small frown." |
| display | How openly the body shows a feeling: 1 contained, 2 visible, 3 open; **still** lists what does not move. | Shot 150: display 1; head, hands and body still. |
| moment | A timed change inside a shot. | Seconds 4 to 6 of shot 150: she stops chewing and frowns. |
| keep hidden | Kept out of the frame until later. | Saye's ring, until the script shows it. |
| saved choice | A strong choice kept for a few moments only. | The mirrored frame of the two women, used in scene 10 and scene 29. |
| purpose | What a shot is for. | Shot 150: Iona's body admits what her words denied. |
| why | The reason a shot leaves the plain way (camera still, at the eye height of the person the scene belongs to, on the normal lens); it names something in your story. | "Her face changes." puts the turn inside her mouth. |
| screen time | How long a shot lasts in the film. | 15 seconds. |
| text in picture | Words the audience reads inside the picture; the drawn file is the text graphic. | The title card, THE CATCH. |

## Light, sound and style

| Word | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| look | The light, colour and texture of a place at one time; its pasted text is the look block. | Saye's kitchen before dawn: one warm lamp, a faint blue window. |
| main light | The light that shapes the faces. | The lamp on the table. |
| style | How the whole film appears, set out in style words. | Photographic, muted colour, a level, steady camera. |
| appearance | How a character appears. | Saye: grey, upright, buttoned to the collar. |
| voice | How a character sounds, set out in a voice description. | Saye: low and even, every word finished. |
| path | How a voice reaches us: in the room, from off the frame, through a phone. | Saye's answer reaches Iona from off the frame. |
| room sound | A place's steady background sound. | The kitchen's hum. |
| emphasis | How strongly a thing is pointed out, from 0 to 3; sound emphasis, for a sound. | The mint, at emphasis 2 at the turn. |

## People and things across the film

| Word | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| fixed description | The same words pasted into every picture prompt for a person, place or thing, so it looks the same each time. | "Dr Saye, a slim, upright woman in her fifties ..." |
| state | How a person or thing looks from one point on: costume, wounds, dirt. Its words are the state line. | Iona, state 2: sleeve torn, palm skinned. |
| continuity | The record of every state, file 09. | Jude, state 3: the shirt cut away. |
| reversed, original | In a mirror story, which way round a person or thing is in the story itself. | In scene 10 Saye and her kitchen are reversed; Iona and the flask are original. |
| mirrored, normal | How a person or thing shows in one shot. | In scene 10 Saye and her kitchen show mirrored; Iona shows normal. |
| era | A stretch of the film where the mirroring stays the same. | The Catch has three. |
| flip | Turning a finished picture left to right in the edit. | The ring inserts are never flipped. |
| whole-film files | Files 05 to 10, which hold what is true for the whole film; file 02 sums them up. | 10 Film rules. |

## Making it with AI

| Word | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| grey previews | Rough grey 3D versions of hard shots, to test where the camera and people go. The AI's own files call them **previs**. | Shot 080's: Iona frame-left, Saye frame-right, Eli small between them. |
| reference pictures | Pictures of one person or thing in one state, so AI tools draw them the same each time; a style picture does this for the whole film. | Iona in state 2, front and side. |
| start picture, end picture | Pictures a video clip starts or ends on. | Shot 150 is made from a start picture of Iona. |
| layout picture | A grey still that fixes where things stand in the frame. | Shot 080's two women and the table. |
| guide video | A grey render an AI video tool copies the motion from. | The fall in scene 6, made first as a grey render. |
| clip length | How long a piece of AI video is made. | Shot 150: 15 seconds on screen, a 17-second clip. |
| take | One try at making a clip; you keep or reject each. | "Take 3 of shot 150: keep it? [keep]" |
| the first estimate, the estimate from the shots | Running time and cost, first from your story's words, later from the shots. | The Catch, first estimate: about 35 minutes. |

## Your part

| Word | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| choice | A question for you with its answer ready; "defaults" takes every ready answer. | Choice 1: is this story yours? [It's mine] |
| small choice | A choice the AI made without asking, listed so you can change it. | Where everyone stands in the kitchen. |
| addition | Something the plan shows that your story does not say, kept small, for you to keep or cut. | Iona sets the lamp down. |
| step | One of the 12 steps of the work. | Step 8 of 12: designing the scenes and listing their shots. |
| depth | How far the plan goes: quick (one line per shot), standard, detailed. | Standard. |
| finding | A problem the checks found, with its fix. | A reason that would fit any film. |
| the scene list, the big choices, each group of shots, the finished check | The four times you are asked to choose. | The big choices: the climax, the style, the music. |
| check chat | A new chat that checks a group of scenes, because an AI does not judge its own work well. | "Check my group of scenes." |
